# élevé by Chandni — Design Bible

## Part 4 — The Comparison Matrix

This part sets all fifteen editions against each other on a single normalised
scale, then compares them section by section, system by system, and finally maps
them to business outcomes.

---

## 4.0 — A note on the rubric

Part 1 (Editions 01–05) was scored on a 15-criterion rubric built for *structural*
directions:

> Luxury · Editorial · Creativity · Originality · Typography · Motion ·
> Photography · UX · Conversion · Accessibility · Performance · Storytelling ·
> Elegance · Premium feel · Craftsmanship

Parts 2 and 3 (Editions 06–15) were scored on a 16-criterion rubric built for
*presentational* directions:

> Luxury feel · Elegance · Uniqueness · Photography · Typography · Colour ·
> Layout · Motion · Whitespace · Detail · Consistency · Emotion · Conversion ·
> Accessibility · Performance · Responsiveness

To compare all fifteen fairly, Part 1's scores are mapped onto the 16-criterion
rubric as follows:

sixteen normalised criteria. They disagree, and the disagreement is itself a
finding — see §4.2.

### Why they diverge

The flat mean **rewards all-rounders and punishes specialists**. Cherry rises to
first because it has two 10s and only one score below 7.5; Editorial falls to
second because Uniqueness (7.0) and Conversion (7.0) drag an otherwise superb page.
Pistachio has the widest spread in the project — **10.0 on four criteria and 5.5 on
Emotion** — which averages it down to 11th despite containing more original ideas
than any other edition.

**Neither ranking should be used alone to pick a winner.** A website is not an
average; it is a set of decisions optimised for one audience. The correct reading
is: *the flat mean tells you which editions have no weaknesses; the holistic score
tells you which editions have the highest ceiling.* The decision guide at §4.9
resolves this against actual business goals.

### Consistency of scoring

- **Mean of all 15 unified means:** 8.47
- **Range:** 7.91 (Citrine) → 8.84 (Cherry) — a spread of only 0.93
- **Standard deviation across editions:** ≈ 0.29

The narrow spread is expected and correct: all fifteen share one content model, one
type stack, one easing curve and one motion philosophy. The real differences are
**not in overall quality but in shape** — which is what the rest of Part 4 maps.

### The five archetypes

Clustering the fifteen by shape rather than score:

**A. The Restrained Classicists** — 01 Editorial, 04 Journal, 14 Pearl, 10 Porcelain
Warm/cool neutrals, hairlines, serif discipline, low motion, high elegance.
*Mean luxury 9.4 · mean emotion 7.8 · mean uniqueness 7.9*

**B. The Dramatists** — 02 Gallery, 15 Cherry
Dark or saturated grounds, full-bleed blocks, big type, high contrast.
*Mean luxury 9.0 · mean conversion 9.25 · mean whitespace 8.25*

**C. The Craft Objects** — 03 Atelier, 09 Pistachio, 12 Riviera, 13 Mocha
The page is a physical artefact. Highest Detail and Consistency in the project.
*Mean detail 9.75 · mean consistency 9.75 · mean photography 6.75*

**D. The Atmospherics** — 08 Lavender, 06 Garden, 11 Citrine
Colour worlds with background systems (orbs, arches, sun discs).
*Mean emotion 8.7 · mean performance 8.2 · mean typography 7.5*

**E. The Romantics** — 07 Blush
A cluster of one. Highest emotion, lowest photography-to-charm ratio.

**Finding: cluster C has the best craft and the worst photography** (mean 6.75 vs
the project's 6.6 — and Atelier alone carries it; without Atelier the cluster mean
falls to 5.8). The object metaphors consistently crowd out the imagery.

### The three shortlists

**Shortlist A — "Safe and excellent" (recommend if the client is undecided)**
01 Editorial · 10 Porcelain · 14 Pearl
*All three are top-four on the unified mean, have no criterion below 6.5, and read

edition, the highest-value final site would be:

- **Base architecture:** 01 Editorial (already componentised, highest holistic score)

- **Footer:** 09 Pistachio's colophon
- **Plus the seven missing content items** from §4.7

This hybrid would score an estimated **9.3–9.5 unified** — meaningfully above any
single edition — because it pairs the project's best structural decisions with its
best palette and closes all seven content gaps.

## 4.10 — Twelve findings to carry into Part 5

1. **Cherry leads the unified mean (8.84); Editorial leads the holistic score
   (9.1).** Neither should decide alone.
2. **The quality spread across fifteen editions is only 0.93** — they differ in
   shape, not standard.
3. **Eleven of fifteen editions win at least one criterion outright.** Porcelain
   wins none yet ranks second — the project's best generalist.
4. **Photography is the systemic weakness.** Mean 6.6 of 22 images; six editions
   use four or fewer; three hide six images each at a breakpoint.
5. **Seven content items are missing from all fifteen editions** — FAQ, booking,
   response time, credentials, social, case studies, legal.
6. **All fifteen pre-fill the enquiry interest field** — the project's most
   consistent good decision.
7. **All fifteen convert by `mailto:` only.**
8. **Four editions cannot reach their own services** without scrolling.
9. **Zero infinite animations across the whole project** — the motion brief was met
   completely.
10. **Luxury and emotion are inversely correlated**, and only four editions score
    ≥ 17.5 combined.
11. **Uppercase display type appears in only three editions**, all of which score
    ≥ 8.0 on uniqueness — the most under-used lever available.
12. **The three most transplantable components** — Pistachio's services table,
    Mocha's receipt, Riviera's two-panel back — are all palette-independent.
